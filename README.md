# Twilite

## Build knowledge that outlives the conversation

Twilite is a visual workspace for ideas, evidence, and plans. Connect what you
learn to what you decide and what you do next. Explore it yourself, or work
with AI on the same connected material.

**[Start at Twilite Zone](https://twilite.zone/?doc=github%3A%2F%2Ftwilite-zone%2Fpublic%2Froot.node)**

New here? [Explore a small workshop plan](https://twilite.zone/?doc=github%3A%2F%2Ftwilite-zone%2Fpublic%2Ftutorial%2Ffirst-graph%2Froot.node),
then [build your first useful graph](https://twilite.zone/?doc=github%3A%2F%2Ftwilite-zone%2Fpublic%2Ftutorial%2Fstart-here%2Froot.node).
Follow the evidence to a decision and its next step. No graph vocabulary is
needed to begin.

A Twilite graph can hold content, relationships, presentation, and behavior in
one portable web artifact. People can explore it visually, while AI can inspect
and evolve the same explicit structure over time.

## Who Twilite is for

Twilite is for people whose work becomes more valuable as its connections grow:

- researchers and investigators preserving evidence and relationships;
- software and product architects coordinating complex systems;
- knowledge-system builders developing durable human-and-AI workspaces; and
- creators managing interconnected worlds, publications, or bodies of work.

## Why a graph?

Most tools flatten relationships or scatter them across files and tabs. Twilite
keeps those relationships visible and addressable. A graph can move from idea,
to collaboration, to execution, to publication without being rebuilt in a new
medium at every step.

Twilite graphs use `.node` files: portable JSON artifacts that can be stored in
Git, published on the web, linked through durable `github://` addresses, and
worked on by both people and agents.

## Explore freely. Save with production access.

Browse public graphs and try authoring freely. Saving requires production
access. In Twilite, open **Document actions → Unlock production tools** for
account options. Once saving is enabled, **Save As…** lets you save to GitHub
or save locally.
